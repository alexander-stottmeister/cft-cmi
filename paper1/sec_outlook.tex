\label{sec:outlook}
\subsection{Universality}
The constants $f_2=c_{\rm cft}/(12\pi^2)$ and $s_2=c_{\rm cft}/12$ of
\cref{prop:gBvalue} were obtained here from the one-particle kernel identity, a
free-fermion computation.  They should be universal, and Note~7 \cite{Note7} gives
the argument for an arbitrary diffeomorphism-covariant conformal net in four
steps:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item the tangent vector of the recovery curve is the derivation by the stress
tensor smeared with the special conformal vector field restricted to the recovered
interval, $\dot\omega(X)=i\omega([T(g),X])$ with a universal $g$
($g=0$ on $A$, $g(x)=-x^2/L$ on $D$);
\item the second variations of fidelity and relative entropy are the Bures and
Kubo--Mori quadratic forms of that tangent, expressed through the modular operator
of the interval,
$g_B=2\langle\eta,(\Delta+1)^{-1}\eta\rangle$,
$g_{\mathrm{KM}}=\langle\eta,\frac{\log\Delta}{\Delta-1}\eta\rangle$ with
$\eta=i(S^*-1)T(g)\Omega$;
\item the modular flow of an interval is geometric, so both reduce to the vacuum
two-point function of the stress tensor in the modular frame, where it is the
thermal function of a weight-two field at inverse temperature $2\pi$;
\item the two spectral integrals evaluate to $g_B=2c_{\rm cft}/(3\pi^2)$ and
$g_{\mathrm{KM}}=c_{\rm cft}/6$, giving \eqref{eq:main} with $c_{\rm cft}$ in
place of $r$.
\end{enumerate}
Every ingredient is fixed by the central charge, which is why the answer is
proportional to $c_{\rm cft}$; the universal number multiplying it is pinned by
the free-fermion case proved here.

Two gaps separate this from a theorem.  First, the type III second-variation
lemma: its Bures half is now proved for every von Neumann algebra with cyclic
separating $\Omega$ and every self-adjoint $A$ with $\Omega\in D(A)$,
$A\Omega\in D(\Delta^{-1/2})$ \cite{RigorSV}, but its Kubo--Mori half is open
beyond finite dimensions.  Second, the zero-collar tangent: for the representative
$g=f\mathbf1_D$ the vector $T(g)\Omega$ has infinite norm, so the lemma must be
applied to a finite-norm representative ($g$ continued smoothly beyond $L$;
different continuations change $T(g)$ by elements of $\cF(I)'$ and leave the
tangent functional unchanged), and the passage to the spectral integrals in the
$\xi$-frame needs a separate form-domain statement.  Monotone convergence
$g_B^{(\eps)}\uparrow g_B$ along increasing algebras is proved; the identification
of the zero-collar tangent functional with the spectral integral is reduced to a
uniform-convergence statement for cut-off tangents and remains open.  An
independent $x$-space route (Mellin transform in $u=L-x$, which diagonalizes the
dilation) reproduces the thermal spectral density from the stress-tensor two-point
function and the geometric dilation alone, so the $\beta=2\pi$ thermal form is a
consequence and not an input; the same route locates the obstruction precisely at
$g(L)\ne0$, i.e.\ in the behaviour of the tangent field at the moving endpoint, not
in the choice of chart.

\subsection{Open problems}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(O\arabic*),leftmargin=2.6em]
\item \emph{A rate for the remainder.}  \Cref{thm:main} gives
$\Phi=f_2\zeta^2+o(\zeta^2)$ with no rate.  The expansion
\eqref{eq:expansion-structure} proceeds in integer powers, so what is missing is a
two-sided $O(\zeta^3)$ remainder.  The upper half is available: the global bound of
\cref{rem:norate} gives $\Phi-f_2\zeta^2\le f_2^2\zeta^4/(1-2f_2\zeta^2)$, whence
$c_3\le0$ once the limit defining $c_3$ exists \cite{RigorRR}.  A matching lower
bound needs a cubic remainder uniform in the modular cutoff.  (The formal
fourth-order Taylor coefficient of the quadratic quantum-Fisher functional
diverges, but that is not the expansion of $\Phi$, whose quartic coefficient is
finite, $c_4f_2\approx+0.0076$.)
\item \emph{The Kubo--Mori half.}  Prove $\cD(\zeta)=s_2\zeta^2+o(\zeta^2)$ for
$\cD$ itself.  The Bures upper bound has no Kubo--Mori analogue, since the
Kubo--Mori weight is not dominated by $1+\lambda$; a substitute would presumably
go through Araki's relative entropy as a supremum over Connes cocycles.
\item \emph{A bosonic check.}  The chiral $\mathrm U(1)$ current algebra at
$c_{\rm cft}=1$ should give the same $f_2$; a quasi-free bosonic computation would
test the universality claim outside the CAR setting and would exercise the
implementability step in its Weyl form.
\item \emph{Optimality.}  \Cref{cor:collapse} shows that the best member of the
rotated family for a single chirality is the pure geometric compression
$\overline\beta_\lambda$.  It is \emph{not} optimal among all normal recovery maps
$\cF_r(AB)\to\cF_r(ABC)$: every recovery channel's recovered symbol is quasi-free
feasible, so the second-order problem over all channels is an explicit convex
programme \cite{RigorAll}; the compression is not its minimiser, a Bogoliubov
rotation inside $B$ lowering the second-order functional by a factor $1-\theta$,
$\theta=0.384\pm0.003$ numerically \cite{RigorS10,RigorF3}; and the corresponding
upper bound on the exact fidelity holds because exterior directions are dense in the
invisible space (\cite{RigorE1}, Theorem~5.3).  The open question is the exact value
of the all-channel optimum, bracketed between the dual value and
$(1-\theta)f_2z^2=0.616\,f_2z^2$.
\item \emph{Beyond the vacuum.}  Thermal states and states on the circle have the
same modular geometry after a conformal map, so the collapse
\eqref{eq:collapse} should persist with $\zeta$ replaced by the appropriate cross
ratio; the recovery error should again be $f_2\zeta^2$ with the same $f_2$.
\item \emph{Large cross ratio.}  Certify $\Phi(\zeta)$ for $\zeta\gtrsim1$ to
$1\%$ (\cref{rem:largezeta}).  The numerics currently give brackets of
$0.45$--$6.6\%$ (\cref{tab:largezeta}) and a local log-slope falling from $1.29$ to
$0.69$; the lattice suggests
$\Phi\sim\zeta^{1.1}$.  The asymptotics of $\Phi$ as $\zeta\to\infty$ is not known
even heuristically.
\item \emph{A chiral lattice test of the collapse.}  \Cref{cor:collapse} is a
single-chirality statement and is therefore not directly visible in a
parity-invariant lattice model.  A chiral or parity-broken model would test it
without the two-branch sum.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Beyond the vacuum and beyond adjacency (lattice results).}
Three lattice experiments with the Gaussian pipeline of \cref{sec:2d} indicate the reach of the quadratic law \cite{RigorS9}.  For the thermal state of the chain the two-branch law holds verbatim with the cross ratio replaced by its cylinder form $\eta_\beta=\sinh(\pi a/\beta_\ell)\sinh(\pi c/\beta_\ell)/[\sinh(\pi(a+b)/\beta_\ell)\sinh(\pi(b+c)/\beta_\ell)]$ (ratio $0.98$--$1.01$ over five decades), and for $L_B\gg\beta$ the error is shielded exponentially, with rate $2\pi$ for $-\log F$ and $\pi$ for the conditional mutual information in units of the thermal length.  Away from criticality, for a staggered mass with $mL_B\gtrsim1.6$, and likewise for a gap between $A$ and $B$, the recovery error becomes \emph{linear} in the conditional mutual information (ratio $0.10$--$0.17$): the quadratic law is a statement about the conformal fixed point and adjacency.  Along the massive flow the Markov $c$-function of \cite{Note3} dominates the entropic one, $c_M\ge c_E$, both decreasing monotonically from $c$ to $0$ with $c_M/c_E\to mR$.

\paragraph{Optimality at second order.} Among quasi-free channels acting on $B$ the second-order recovery error is an exact semidefinite programme in the one-particle data \cite{RigorS8}, and \emph{every} normal unital completely positive even recovery channel has a recovered symbol in its feasible set (Kadison--Schwarz, the Tomita operator of $\cF_r(B)$, and the one-particle restriction of the modular objects), so the all-channel second-order problem has the same value \cite{RigorAll}.  The zero-collar compression is not the minimiser: composing it with a Bogoliubov rotation inside $B$ lowers the second-order functional by the factor $1-\theta$, $\theta=0.384\pm0.003$ from two independent discretisations that agree to $3\cdot10^{-4}$ after extrapolation, the modular Galerkin frame \cite{RigorAll} and a parameter-free modular Wiener--Hopf frame \cite{RigorF3}; the smaller value $\theta\ge0.30$ of the spectral circle model \cite{RigorS10} (reproduced with the optimised exterior extension in \cite{RigorE2}) is a lower bound, its ultraviolet taper suppressing modes that carry about a fifth of $\theta$ \cite{RigorF3}.  The Uhlmann bound with an optimised purification on the exterior of $ABC$ turns this into an upper bound on the exact fidelity, because the exterior directions exhaust the invisible space \cite{RigorE1}: per chirality $E_{\rm rec}\le(1-\theta_{\rm fr})f_2z^2\,(1+o(1))$, with $\theta_{\rm fr}>0$ iff the $D$-block of the Riesz vector of the compression direction is not self-adjoint (proved) and $\theta_{\rm fr}\ge0.38$ (numerical).  The exact optimum is bracketed between the dual value of the programme and $0.616\,f_2z^2$; the Petz map, at $4f_2z^2$, is at least $5.6$ times worse than the best channel.
