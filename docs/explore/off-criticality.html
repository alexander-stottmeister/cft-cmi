Module 8 — Off criticality: gap, temperature, mass

The quadratic law is a statement about adjacent intervals at a conformal fixed point. Open a gap, heat the state, or turn on a mass, and you can watch which parts survive. Heating is a conformal change of state and the law survives it, with the cross ratio transported; a gap or a mass is not, and there the recovery error becomes linear in the conditional mutual information.

proved The gap law itself is a closed form

With a gap G between A and B, traced out, the massless Dirac two-interval entropies give exactly the adjacent-interval formula with LB → LB + LG: I(A:C|B) = (1/3) log[(a+g+b)(g+b+c) / ((g+b)(a+g+b+c))] ~ (1/3) L_A L_C / L_G². Every log LG term cancels. Your browser evaluates this formula live; the lattice confirms it to 0.06% at LG = 96.

Where this comes from: the adjacent-interval CMI · the gap claim · rigor/thermal_rg_gap_summary.md

numerical Separated intervals: the recovery error decays with the same power

If the quadratic law held, −log F would go like LG−4. It does not: it decays like LG−2, so the ratio −log F / I tends to a constant near 0.09, independent of the interval length. Recovery is only quadratic in the CMI at a conformal fixed point with adjacency.

Where this comes from: claim page · numerics/lattice/petz_thermal_rg.py (output petz_thermal_rg.out, section 3)

numerical Thermal states: the vacuum law at the cylinder cross ratio, and exponential shielding

KMS states obey the vacuum two-branch law at the thermal cross ratio eta_beta = sinh(pi a/bl) sinh(pi c/bl) / [sinh(pi(a+b)/bl) sinh(pi(b+c)/bl)], bl = 2 beta, the vacuum cross ratio of the images of the four endpoints under the exponential map. For LB ≫ β the error is shielded exponentially, at rate 2π in −log F and π in the CMI, in thermal-length units (v = 2 sites per unit lattice time, so ξ = β/π sites).

numerical The Markov c-function along the massive flow

With cM(R) = 3R² IR−2(2)/4, a genuine lattice CMI and so non-negative by strong subadditivity, and cE(R) = 3R (S(R+2) − S(R−2))/4: both fall monotonically from 1 to 0 along the flow, cM ≥ cE everywhere as the entropic c-theorem requires, and cM/cE → mR in the infrared — so cM is not an independent infrared quantity, it is cE times the RG time.

A 2–3% overshoot cM > 1 at R = 6, 8 does not collapse between m = 0.05 and m = 0.1 at fixed μR, so it is a lattice artefact of the smallest admissible δ = 2, not evidence against monotonicity. The curves below are measurements on a finite chain, not a theorem.
