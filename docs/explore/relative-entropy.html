Module 9 — Relative entropy: the conjecture and its bracket

The fidelity coefficient is a theorem. The relative-entropy coefficient is not. Two independent measurements land on c/12 to about one per cent, and the best bracket anyone here can prove around them is a factor of twenty-seven wide — and even its two ends are conditional, on a hypothesis that is open and on a source that was never obtained.

conjectural s₂ = c/12 for a general net

Along the zero-collar recovery curve ωs = ω ∘ Ad Us, the conjecture is s2 = lim D(omega_s || omega)/s² =? c/12. Believed, consistent with both measurements, and not proved for any net. This is the one headline number of the programme that is not a theorem.

conditional The bracket, and what each end depends on

Neither end of 0.0338c ≤ liminf ≤ limsup ≤ 0.924c is unconditional.

Upper end, 0.924c: proved under hypothesis (V), uniform integrability along the orbit. (V) is open — Gap RK1 — and is known only at σ = 0. Two hypotheses of the earlier route, (Uε) and (Aε), are refuted for the big interval algebra: locality in K forbids them.
Lower end, 0.0338c: proved modulo Araki's perturbation expansion, which is hypothesis (ii) of the lower-bound theorem. The locators are Araki's 1973 paper and Bratteli–Robinson II §5.4.1; neither was opened here (NOT OBTAINED), so it is carried as a hypothesis and not as a checked citation. That is Gap RK2, and it is recorded rather than glossed.

So the honest reading of the bracket is: conditional at both ends, with named hypotheses. It is still worth having — it is what makes the second variation finite at all — but it is not the same kind of statement as Theorem A.

refuted The Kubo–Mori gauge lemma by restriction to A(K)

What was claimed. The Phase-2b route to s₂ = c/12 had three steps: (a) choose an interval K ⊇ I that also carries the implementer, so that Ad Us is an automorphism of A(K) and monotonicity applies; (b) evaluate the result to second order; (c) the gauge lemma: the infimum of the Kubo–Mori form over admissible gauges is c/6.

What is true. Steps (a) and (b) are correct. Step (c) is false. The infimum exists, is attained in the limit by an explicit one-parameter family, and equals (11+5√5)c/12 at R* = (3+√5)/2 — a factor 11.09 above c/6.

Why. In the modular frame of any interval K ⊋ I the Kubo–Mori form is the local Sobolev functional g_KM(T(f)Omega) = (c/12) integral [f''² + f'²] dxi, and the fixed part of the field — gtot = −x² on the compressed interval, which no gauge may alter — already costs 11 + 5√5 units, where c/6 corresponds to 2. Note 7's c/6 was the analytic continuation of a divergent integral. Kosaki and Petz–Donald give suprema, so they bound from below only.

This card is kept because the record is part of the result: the route was tried, the constant it needed is false by a factor of 11.09, and what survives is the finite bracket above, not the conjecture.

proved The exact identity on unitary orbits — and why it does not apply here

For ωs = ω(e−isA · eisA) with A affiliated with the algebra, D(omega || omega_s) = || h(Delta)^(1/2) (e^{isA} - 1) Omega ||², h(lambda) = lambda - 1 - log lambda. It gives the lower bound unconditionally and the full second-order expansion under modular analyticity. It is not applicable to the recovery curve, whose generator is not affiliated with the interval algebra — which is exactly why the gauge route was tried at all.
